Treatment of Experimental Data and Visualizing Uncertainty

Students will learn how to treat experimental data of gas pressure and volume and approximate uncertainties using various techniques.

Programming Skill: None
Primary Course: Physical Chemistry Lab
Also for: Physical Chemistry, Analytical Chemistry
Format: Multi-Part Module
Authors: Prof. Prajay Patel
Estimated Time: 3-4 hours
Piloted with: Undergraduate - Third/Fourth Year
Students Tested: 10
python pandas uncertainty lab monte-carlo visualization gas-laws

Overview

How can you import your data from Excel into Python? How do I choose and create the correct plot? What are different ways to represent experimental uncertainty? What is the extent of data needed for proper calibration? This notebook explores various methods to measure and visualize uncertainty through graphical error, best fit line uncertainties, and Monte Carlo sampling of the confidence interval. The sample data provided is air pressure in a closed plastic syringe altering the volume from 20 mL to 10 mL.

Scientific Learning Objectives

  • Compute a linear regression
  • Visualize uncertainty
  • Differentiate between best fit, graphical, and Monte Carlo methods for error estimation
  • Discuss Monte Carlo sampling in context of experimental error
  • Calculate propagation of errors efficiently
  • What are different ways to represent experimental uncertainty?
  • What is the extent of data needed for proper calibration?

Cyberinfrastructure Learning Objectives

  • Write and execute Python code in Jupyter notebooks
  • Visualize multiple types of plots in matplotlib with legends
  • Implement scipy.stats.linregress() for linear regression
  • How can you import your data from Excel into Python?
  • How do I choose and create the correct plot?

Instructor Notes

Additional context for instructors using this lesson:

• Different uncertainty levels may take a while to grasp. This includes the graphical error.
• The Monte Carlo graphs will take a while to render because there are 50000 data points being plotted.
• The instructor version works in both ChemCompute and Colab; however, the version in Colab will not render the markdown JupyterMyST colored cells.

Timing notes:
• Part 1 typically takes students 30-60 minutes.
• Part 2 can be rushed in 2 hours but is better with 3 hours.

Common student questions:
• "Why isn't this working?" • good opportunity to remind students of patience that comes with programming
