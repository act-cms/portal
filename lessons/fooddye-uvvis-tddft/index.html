Computational UV-vis Spectroscopy of Food Dyes

Use TDDFT in Turbomole/TmoleX on an HPC cluster to predict the UV-vis spectra and colors of three candidate molecules and identify which one is the Red-40 dye by comparison to experiment.

Programming Skill: None
Primary Course: Foundational Module
Also for: Physical Chemistry, Analytical Chemistry, Materials Science
Format: Single Notebook
Authors: Prof. Robin Grotjahn
Estimated Time: 1.5-2 hours
Piloted with: Undergraduate - First Year
Students Tested: 22
tddft uv-vis-spectroscopy dft computational-chemistry excited-states turbomole tmolex hpc conjugation dyes

Overview

Food dyes are colored because they absorb specific wavelengths of visible light. In this lesson students use time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT) in the Turbomole program, driven through the TmoleX GUI and run on a high-performance computing (HPC) cluster, to predict the UV-vis spectra and colors of three candidate structures (A, B, C) and decide which one is the Red-40 (Allura Red) dye, whose absorption maximum in aqueous solution lies near 504 nm.

Along the way students set up a realistic solution-phase calculation: a def2-TZVPD basis set, a molecular charge of -2, and COSMO implicit solvation for water. They submit the job to a cluster queue through Open OnDemand, compute the spectrum and predicted color, and compare against experimental reference data. The lesson mirrors how chemists screen candidate molecules computationally before committing to synthesis, and connects the size of a π-conjugated system to absorption wavelengths. It can be paired with instructor chosen wet-lab components such as quantitative analysis via Beer's law. No programming is required and the workflow is entirely GUI-based.

Scientific Learning Objectives

  • Explain electronic excitation as the molecular origin of UV-vis absorption and visible color.
  • Select calculation settings (basis set, charge, functional, implicit solvation).
  • Predict a molecule's visible color from its computed UV-vis spectrum.
  • Compare computed spectra and colors with experimental reference data to identify a compound.
  • Relate the size of a π-conjugated system to its absorption wavelength.

Cyberinfrastructure Learning Objectives

  • Log in to an HPC cluster
  • Launch a remote desktop session and operate a graphical application (TmoleX) within it.
  • Set up and run a quantum-chemical (TDDFT) calculation through a GUI.
  • Configure remote systems and submit a job to a cluster queue (queued, multi-threaded execution).
  • Manage remote files and terminate the interactive session to free shared resources.

Instructor Notes

Piloted in CHEM-14L (Advanced Chemical Principles Laboratory, general
chemistry lab for first-year students) at Santa Clara University, with one
instructor and one TA for 22 students in a single lab session. Budget about
1.5-2 hours of computational work.

Software and access:
• Requires Turbomole 8.0 + the integrated TmoleX GUI (a free educational
license is available at https://store.turbomole.org/product/turbomole-8-0-educational/;
note that this is not an academic research license).
• Calculations are more demanding and requests ~48 threads to complete in 20-30 minutes.
Queued execution on an HPC cluster via Open OnDemand is
recommended over individual laptops.

Input files (in material/): A.xyz, B.xyz, C.xyz, and remote-configuration.xml.
The XML is a SLURM template with UPPERCASE placeholders (partition, scratch
path, TURBODIR, Turbomole version, mail domain); edit it for your site before
distributing. Students copy the inputs to /tmp and enter their own cluster
credentials in TmoleX after import.

Calculation settings (must match across all three structures): basis
def2-TZVPD; charge -2; functional PBE; COSMO solvation with epsilon = 80.4
and refractive index = 1.33 (water); UV/Vis and CD vertical excitations,
40 excitations, All IRREPs; run queued on the remote node (48 threads),
not local. PBE was chosen deliberately. More sophisticated functionals such
as the range-separated hybrid ωB97X-D actually perform worse for this
particular system or do not separate the different systems in the same
pedagocically useful manner that's achieved at the PBE/def2-TZVPD (COSMO) level.
